    <h2>Piano</h2>
    <p>When I am not coding or observing, I play the piano. I love <b>Chopin</b>, especially the études and the nocturnes. The études are a long-term project: most of them are so difficult and take so long to play well that I haven't started a new one for a while. The one I play fairly well is <b>Op. 10, No. 12, the &ldquo;Revolutionary&rdquo; Étude</b>. I also enjoy <b>Beethoven's piano concertos</b>, which are easier to play fluently.</p>

    <h2>Why I do science</h2>
    <p>I get bored quickly when a task is not intellectually challenging enough. Research is one of the very few things that keeps being interesting and rewarding, always produces something new, and costs almost nothing. That is also why I am not a big fan of traveling and conferences: between all the logistics and complications, I would rather spend the time on a hard problem.</p>

  <div class="interest__body">
    <h2>On AI</h2>
    <p>I am thrilled by the almost daily progress of artificial intelligence, and I am something of an AI accelerationist: I think it is only a matter of time before AI enables groundbreaking scientific discoveries. That's not because AI <em>uniquely</em> enables any discovery. It's because AI has made testing an idea so cheap that ideas can quickly be turned into results. As an average astronomer, I am not a great programmer, and I am very happy to see AI make the tedious programming so much easier. (This website was built with help from an AI, too.)</p>
