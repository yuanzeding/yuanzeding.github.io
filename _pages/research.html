I am interested in how supermassive black holes and their host galaxies grow together, from the physics of the inner accretion disk to the gas in the circumgalactic medium. I combine high-energy (X-ray) spectroscopy and timing with optical and near-infrared integral-field spectroscopy, and I contribute to the software used to reduce and model these data.

The circumgalactic medium of AGN at cosmic noon

Preliminary Thesis project, paper about to be submitted

Ding, Steidel, Harrison et al., The Hosting Environment of Active Galaxies at Redshift 2–3. I. The Nature of the Extended Lyα Nebulae around AGNs (in prep.). The results below come from a draft that has not yet been refereed, so details may change.

At z ~ 2–3, galaxies and black holes are growing fastest, and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) is where inflows, outflows and feedback leave their mark. Over the past year I have used deep Keck/KCWI integral-field spectroscopy of 55 AGN from the Keck Baryonic Structure Survey (KBSS) to map their extended Lyα nebulae, adding Keck/MOSFIRE rest-optical spectroscopy for many of them. The sample spans about 8 magnitudes in AGN luminosity: hyperluminous QSOs, optically faint broad-line AGN, and type-2 AGN. This lets me ask how the gas around a galaxy responds as the black hole's ionizing output changes by a factor of about 1000.

Nebulae are everywhere, but scale weakly with AGN power. Extended Lyα is detected around every hyperluminous QSO and type-2 AGN and around most faint type-1 AGN. The line is usually a single redshifted peak with FWHM ≈ 500–700 km s-1. Nebular luminosity grows much more slowly than AGN luminosity: a factor of ~103 more ionizing output gives only a factor of ~15 more Lyα. This is hard to explain if the CGM were fully ionized and glowing by recombination.

Resonant scattering in a clumpy, partly neutral CGM. For nearly all AGN fainter than Mi ≈ −24, Lyα is broader and redder than the narrow Balmer lines, which requires substantial resonant scattering. Together with the weak luminosity scaling, this points to dense neutral clumps that survive where the AGN cannot fully ionize them. Around the hyperluminous QSOs the gas instead looks fully ionized, with kinematics consistent with motion in the QSO's potential.

Type-2s look like a gas-rich phase, not just a viewing angle. At similar continuum brightness, type-2 AGN have brighter, more symmetric, more compact and consistently redshifted nebulae than faint type-1s. Combined with their X-ray luminosities, compact hosts and near-Eddington accretion, this favors an obscured, gas-rich evolutionary phase over orientation alone.

No clear feedback signature on CGM scales. Lyα line widths do not decrease with radius. I also show that measuring line widths only over S/N-thresholded voxels ties the width to surface brightness, which may explain some previously reported luminosity–dispersion correlations.

Galaxy mergers and hidden black holes

Published · ApJ 2026

Triple mergers of massive galaxies are a natural step in hierarchical galaxy assembly, and they may produce triple black hole systems that emit gravitational waves. Few have been confirmed. In UGC 2369S, a nearby luminous infrared galaxy, I combined Chandra X-ray, Keck/OSIRIS adaptive-optics, Keck/KCWI, VLT/MUSE, VLA and HST data to study one of the clearest kpc-scale triple-nucleus systems known.

The northern core probably hosts the most massive black hole (MBH ~ 108 M☉). It fits a heavily obscured AGN (AV > 5, NH ≳ 1025 cm-2), but the identification is not yet conclusive. The two other cores show tidal disruption, and the changing three-body potential seems to have stripped the stellar bulge of the southwestern core.

Ongoing I am following this up with spatially resolved stellar-population fitting (pPXF/GIST with a hierarchical, CAPIVARA-style spatial binning) of the KCWI cube.

X-ray reflection and the inner accretion disk

Published · ApJ 2024

The X-ray spectra of accreting black holes carry a reflection signature (the Fe K line and the Compton hump) that encodes black hole spin, disk inclination and the geometry of the corona. Disks around black holes can reach densities of 1015–1022 cm-3, where plasma effects matter, but most reflection models have ignored them.

Working with J. García, T. Kallman and collaborators, the developers of XSTAR and XILLVER, I added high-density corrections to the atomic rates these codes use. Modified recombination rates cause most of the change. Dense disks can produce strong Fe and O resonance features even when highly ionized, which could explain the supersolar iron abundances reported for many sources.

How reliable are reflection-based inclinations? With R. Du, L. Ho and R. Li, we used multi-epoch XMM-Newton + NuSTAR data and mock spectra to show that broadband reflection spectroscopy can recover the inner-disk inclination, especially when reflection is strong (Du et al. 2024). We then compared these inclinations with broad-line-region inclinations from velocity-resolved reverberation mapping. The two are strongly correlated, with a nearly linear relation that suggests the BLR is aligned with the accretion disk (Du et al. 2025).

I also apply these models to black hole X-ray binaries, for example the NuSTAR + NICER reflection spectrum of MAXI J1803−298.

Super-Eddington accretion and AGN outflows

Published · ApJ 2022

Using a long simultaneous XMM-Newton + NuSTAR campaign on the narrow-line Seyfert 1 I Zwicky 1, I followed a series of X-ray flares as they happened. The corona cooled from ~200 to ~15 keV during the flare, likely because of runaway pair production in a compact, disk-like corona.

I found several variable, blueshifted absorbers (ionized winds and ultrafast outflows), one of which may have been launched just after the flare. Their ionization and velocity follow the relation expected for a super-Eddington wind. Their momentum-driven feedback can explain why the black hole in I Zw 1 is undermassive relative to its host bulge.

Supermassive black hole binaries in blazars

As part of the OVRO 40m blazar monitoring collaboration led by A. Readhead, I work on blazars whose light curves vary sinusoidally, a possible sign of a supermassive black hole binary. PKS 2131−021 shows coherent sinusoidal variations from radio to optical frequencies, and PKS J0805−0111 is a second highly significant case. I bring the X-ray side to this work: how the high-energy emission behaves along the binary orbit.

Neutron star X-ray binaries (undergraduate work)

At Wuhan University, working with Prof. Wei Wang, I studied accreting X-ray pulsars with Insight-HXMT. In the high-mass X-ray binary GX 301−2 (left), I found two cyclotron resonant scattering features that track luminosity, rising below ~1037 erg s-1 and falling above it. This implies a surface field of ~1013 G. For 4U 0115+63 (right), I brought wavelet analysis to HXMT timing and found mHz quasi-periodic oscillations. Related work covers GX 301−2 as a nearly orthogonal rotator, Vela X-1, MAXI J1535−571 and GRO J1008−57.
